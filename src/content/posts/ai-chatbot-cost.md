## How self-serve chatbot pricing works

Self-serve tools bill on one of four units, and each one rewards a different kind of business.

- **Per resolution or outcome.** Intercom charges from $0.99 per Fin outcome. An outcome counts when the customer confirms the issue is resolved, when they do not ask for more help after Fin replies, or when Fin completes a workflow, including a hand-off; you are charged once per conversation at most ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)). So an outcome is broader than a confirmed fix: a conversation where the customer does not ask for more help after Fin's reply also counts.
- **Per seat plus resolutions.** Zendesk bills per agent seat and includes 5 (Suite Team) or 10 (Suite Professional) automated resolutions per agent a month; extra automated resolutions are listed at $1.50 each committed or $2.00 pay as you go, and you pay only for requests the AI resolves without a human ([Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/pricing/)).
- **Per conversation.** Tidio counts AI and human conversations against one quota: 50 on Starter at $33 a month, 100 to 1,000 on Growth from $63 a month. Every account gets 50 free AI conversations once, and they do not renew ([Tidio](https://www.tidio.com/pricing/)).
- **Per message credit.** Chatbase sells message credits: 4,000 a month on Basic at $150 ($120 billed yearly), 15,000 on Business at $500 ($400 billed yearly), with extra credits at $40 per 1,000, $99 a month to remove its branding and $25 a month per extra agent ([Chatbase](https://www.chatbase.co/pricing)). A conversation uses several messages, so credits cover fewer conversations than the number suggests.

These are fair, transparent models. A self-serve tool is the better choice when your questions are simple and well covered by your help center, your volume is modest, you already run the help desk it belongs to, and someone on your team has a few hours a week to maintain it.

## What a WhatsApp chatbot costs

A WhatsApp chatbot has two bills: the bot, and Meta's fees for messages sent through the WhatsApp Business Platform. Since July 1, 2025, Meta charges per delivered message instead of per conversation. Messages from customers to you are free. Rates depend on the message category (such as marketing, utility, authentication or service) and on the country code of the person you message, and Meta publishes them in rate cards by currency ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/)).

Two changes took effect on October 1, 2026, according to the same documentation:

- **Service messages** (free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window) are free for the first 1,000 delivered per business phone number each month, and charged from the 1,001st.
- **Utility templates sent in reply to a customer**, such as an order update inside an open service window, are charged again; they had been free since July 2025.

Marketing templates, such as cart reminders and promotions, are charged per delivered message at the rate for each country. When a customer messages you from a click-to-WhatsApp ad in the mobile app, a free entry point window of up to 7 days opens, in which Meta does not charge for marketing, utility, authentication or service messages ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/)). So the WhatsApp chatbot cost depends less on how many questions you answer and more on how many messages you start. A store that mainly replies to customers pays little in Meta fees; a store that sends cart reminders to every opted-in shopper pays per message, per country.

With our [WhatsApp Agent](/ai-agents/whatsapp-ai-agent/), a fair-use volume of messaging is included in the monthly fee and usage above it is passed through at cost. Avoid unofficial WhatsApp tools that skip the Business Platform: they look cheaper, but they put your number at risk of being banned.

## What the AI model costs per conversation

The language model is usually the smallest line on the bill. Providers charge per million tokens (a token is roughly a short word), with separate prices for input (your instructions, retrieved help articles and the chat so far) and output (the bot's reply).

As a worked assumption, not an average: a six-turn support chat where each turn sends 4,000 input tokens and returns 300 output tokens uses 24,000 input and 1,800 output tokens in total. Without prompt caching, that costs:

| Model | Published price per million tokens (input / output) | Model cost of that chat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 5.5 (prompts up to 100,000 tokens) | $0.10 / $0.50 | About $0.003 | [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) |
| Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | $0.30 / $2.50 | About $0.012 | [Google](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash (price through December 31, 2026; $1.50 / $7.50 from January 1, 2027) | $0.75 / $3.75 | About $0.025 | [Google](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 | $2 / $10 | About $0.066 | [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) |

So the model costs between a fraction of a cent and a few cents per conversation, depending on the model and how much text each turn carries. Caching, shorter prompts and routing easy questions to a small model lower it further. What costs real money is everything around the model: the knowledge base, integrations, testing, hand-off and the person who reads transcripts and fixes wrong answers. That is also why a per-resolution price can be many times the model cost: you are paying for the platform and its upkeep, not the tokens.

## What drives the cost of an AI chatbot?

Six things move the price far more than the choice of model. They apply to self-serve tools, managed agents and custom builds alike.

1. **Channels.** Website chat alone is the simplest. Each extra channel (email, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, inside your app) adds setup, testing and, for WhatsApp, Meta's message fees.
2. **Integrations.** A bot that only answers questions reads your help content. A bot that looks up orders, starts returns or books appointments needs secure access to your store, help desk, CRM or calendar, plus rules for what it may change. This is usually the largest part of setup.
3. **Knowledge base.** Clean, current help articles make a cheap bot good. Scattered PDFs, old policies and answers that live only in your team's heads make any bot bad until someone writes them down.
4. **Languages.** Each language needs testing on real phrasing, including slang and messages that mix languages, such as Arabic with English or Roman Urdu.
5. **Volume.** Per-resolution, per-conversation and per-credit plans grow in step with your traffic. Flat fees with a fair-use volume do not, until you pass the volume.
6. **Human hand-off.** A good hand-off passes the transcript, the customer details and a summary into the tool your team already uses. Setting that up, and deciding what must always reach a person, takes time on every option.

## The hidden costs of a chatbot

The plan price is rarely the full bill. Count these before you compare:

- **Your team's setup time:** writing and checking the knowledge base, configuring hand-off, testing edge cases. On a self-serve tool this is all yours.
- **Ongoing tuning:** someone reads transcripts every week, fixes wrong answers and adds missing articles. A chatbot nobody maintains gets worse as your prices and policies change.
- **Help desk seats:** some AI add-ons need a paid help desk plan for the humans who take the hand-offs.
- **Add-ons:** branding removal, extra bots, extra storage, extra credits or premium integrations are often priced separately (the Chatbase add-ons above are one example).
- **Wrong answers:** a bot that invents a refund policy costs more than its subscription. Insist on answers from approved sources only, with an "I don't know" path to a person.
- **Switching cost:** if your knowledge base and flows live inside one vendor's builder, moving later means rebuilding them.

## How to compare: cost per resolved conversation

Each option bills on a different unit, so convert all of them to the cost of one conversation the bot resolves without a person. Use your own numbers.

> Cost per resolved conversation = (monthly fee + usage + your team's time to maintain it) ÷ conversations resolved by the bot in the month

Count only conversations the bot truly resolved, not those it handed off or the ones where the customer gave up. Ask each vendor how they define a resolution, because definitions differ.

As a worked assumption, not an average: suppose 1,000 chats a month, and the bot fully resolves 600. On Intercom's published rate of $0.99 per outcome, 600 outcomes cost about $594 a month, plus seats if your team uses Intercom's help desk, and more if conversations where the customer did not ask for more help after Fin's reply also count as outcomes ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)). On Zendesk Suite Professional with three agents, 30 resolutions are included and the other 570 cost about $855 at the $1.50 committed rate, or $1,140 pay as you go, on top of the seats ([Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/pricing/)).

For a flat-fee managed agent, the per-resolution cost falls as volume grows: divide our monthly fee by your resolved conversations, and add any usage above the fair-use volume. For a custom build, spread the one-off cost over the months you expect to run it and add the running cost. RaftLabs puts ongoing costs for most production chatbots at $400 to $6,000 a month, covering model fees, hosting and databases, and monitoring and improvement ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/chatbot-development-cost)).

Then weigh what a resolution is worth: an order question answered at night instead of next morning, a cart recovered, a support hour freed. If that value is larger than the cost, the chatbot pays for itself.

## Which option should a small business choose?

- **Choose a self-serve tool** if your questions are simple, your help center is in good shape, volume is modest and you have time to maintain it yourself. It is the cheapest way to start.
- **Choose per-resolution AI on your existing help desk** if you already run Intercom or Zendesk and want AI inside the same inbox. Watch the bill as volume grows.
- **Choose a managed agent** if you want it set up on your tools, tested on your real tickets and tuned every week, at a flat monthly fee. This suits online stores and [e-commerce](/industries/ecommerce/) brands with order questions on several channels, and [SaaS](/industries/saas-startups/) teams with steady tier-1 tickets.
- **Choose a custom build** if the chatbot must live inside your product, serve staff, connect to unusual systems or meet strict data rules.
