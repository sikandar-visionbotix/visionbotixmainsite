## How are AI phone minutes billed?

Every AI phone call uses three paid services: telephony (the phone line), speech (turning voice into text and back) and a language model. Platforms bill these per minute. Retell, a widely used voice platform, lists its voice engine at $0.055 a minute and telephony at $0.015 a minute, with the language model priced separately by model, and gives $0.07 to $0.31 a minute as the pay-as-you-go range for voice agents ([Retell AI pricing](https://www.retellai.com/pricing)).

The advertised rate is rarely the full bill. Callsy's June 2026 breakdown puts the real all-in cost at about $0.13 to $0.31 a minute on Retell and about $0.14 to $0.33 on Vapi once the model and telephony are added, and notes that agencies that mark up usage resell it at $0.50 to $2.00 a minute ([Callsy](https://www.callsy.ai/insights/ai-voice-agent-cost-2026)).

## How to compare: cost per answered call

Monthly prices are hard to compare because each option bills on a different unit: callers, calls, minutes or hours. Convert each one to the cost of one answered call with your own numbers.

**For an AI receptionist:**

> Cost per call = (monthly fee + usage above fair use) ÷ calls answered in the month

**For a per-minute platform you run yourself:**

> Cost per call = average call length in minutes × all-in rate per minute, plus your own time to build, test and maintain it

As a worked assumption (not an average): a three-minute call at Callsy's all-in range of $0.13 to $0.31 a minute costs about $0.39 to $0.93 in platform usage, before anyone's time to set it up or fix it ([Callsy](https://www.callsy.ai/insights/ai-voice-agent-cost-2026)).

**For an in-house receptionist:**

> Cost per call = (hourly wage × paid hours + employer costs + cover for leave) ÷ calls answered

Two things change the answer more than the price itself:

1. **Calls a person cannot take.** One receptionist answers one call at a time and only during a shift. An AI receptionist answers several calls at once, at night and at weekends. Count the calls that go to voicemail today, because those have a cost per call too: the booking you lose.
2. **What a call is worth.** Multiply missed calls by the share you think would have booked (use your own booking rate) and by the value of a booking. If that number is larger than the monthly fee, the receptionist pays for itself, whatever the per-call math says.

## Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

On cover hours, almost always. A US receptionist earns a median of $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics figures for May 2025, before benefits and the cover you need for breaks, holidays and sick days ([US BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm)). In the UK the legal floor alone, the National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over, is £12.71 an hour from April 2026 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates)). That is a minimum, not a typical receptionist wage, and employer costs come on top.

A human receptionist is still the better choice when:

- **the call is the service.** Grief, complaints, bad news and delicate conversations need a person, and the AI should hand them over, not handle them;
- **call volume is very low.** If your existing team answers every call today without strain, an AI receptionist mainly adds after-hours cover. Check that missed calls are a real problem first;
- **the front desk does more than phones.** Greeting visitors, taking payments and handling paperwork are not phone work. The best setup is often both: the AI takes overflow and after-hours calls, your receptionist handles the room;
- **your callers will not talk to an AI.** Some practices serve patients who hang up on any automated voice. Forward only after-hours calls at first and watch what happens.

## AI receptionist or answering service?

An answering service with people is a fair middle option. Plans like Smith.ai's human receptionists bill per call, from $300 a month for 30 calls ([Smith.ai](https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists)), and Ruby bills by the minute, from $250 a month for 50 minutes ([Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/pricing/)). They handle unusual calls well, but per-call pricing grows with every call, and calendar booking is often a paid add-on or limited to the scheduling tools they support.

Self-serve AI apps sit at the other end. They are the cheapest monthly option and a good fit if you are comfortable setting up the knowledge base, booking rules and integrations yourself, and checking calls every week. Choose a managed receptionist when you would rather pay once for that work and have someone tune it from real calls.

## How do I choose the right AI receptionist?

Answer these five questions before you compare prices:

- **How many calls, and when?** Count a normal week, including the calls you miss. Low volume suits a self-serve app; high volume or long calls make per-minute and per-call billing expensive.
- **Which booking system?** The receptionist should book into your calendar or practice system during the call, not take a message. Check that your system is supported, such as Open Dental or Cliniko for [dental clinics](/industries/dental-clinics/).
- **After-hours only, or every call?** Many businesses start with after-hours and overflow forwarding and widen it once they trust the agent.
- **Which languages?** English is standard. If callers use Spanish, Arabic, Urdu or other languages, ask whether the agent switches when the caller does, and test it.
- **What must always reach a person?** Write the escalation rules first: urgent symptoms in [healthcare](/industries/healthcare/), a buyer ready to make an offer in [real estate](/industries/real-estate/), a complaint anywhere. A good provider builds the transfers around those rules.

Data rules matter too. For US health data, we deploy on providers that sign a BAA. For UK and Australian businesses, we keep call data in a local cloud region and document the data flow for your privacy lead.
