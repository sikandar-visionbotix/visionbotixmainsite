## What you actually pay for

Every AI agent has two costs: building it and running it.

**Setup** covers the work before the agent talks to a customer:

- mapping the job, the edge cases and the moments a human must step in;
- connecting your tools: phone line, calendar, CRM, helpdesk, inbox or database;
- loading and checking your knowledge: prices, policies, opening hours, product data;
- guardrails: what it may say, what it may never do, which actions need an approval;
- testing on real examples until it handles them, then a supervised go-live.

**Running** covers the work after launch. Models change, your prices change, customers ask questions nobody planned for. Someone has to watch conversations, fix what goes wrong and keep the integrations alive. That is why a well-run agent has a monthly fee, and why a cheap one-off build often stops working within months.

## What a custom AI agent costs

A custom agent makes sense when no packaged agent fits the job: a multi-step process across your own systems, an agent that acts inside your product, or a workflow with strict approvals. With us, [custom AI agent development](/services/ai-agent-development/) starts from **{{project:customAgent}}** and is quoted as a fixed price after scoping, paid by milestone.

What pushes a custom agent up from that starting point:

1. **Integrations.** Each system the agent reads from or writes to needs access rules, error handling and tests. An agent with one integration and an agent with eight are different projects.
2. **Autonomy.** An agent that drafts for a human is cheaper than one that acts alone, because acting alone needs more guardrails, approvals and monitoring.
3. **Knowledge.** Answering from a few clean pages is quick. Answering from thousands of documents in several formats needs retrieval (RAG), evaluation sets and access control.
4. **Channels and languages.** Voice adds telephony and speech. Each extra language needs its own testing.
5. **Compliance.** Health, finance and legal data bring audit logs, data residency, redaction and sign-off steps.
6. **Volume.** High volume changes the hosting and the model choice, and it makes cost per conversation matter.

## What it costs to run an AI agent every month

Our monthly fee ({{agents-monthly-range}} for packaged agents) includes hosting, monitoring, weekly tuning, guardrail updates and a fair-use volume of messages, calls and model usage. Usage above the fair-use volume is passed through at cost, with no markup.

Development companies publish much wider running costs for custom agents. Azilen puts the monthly total for production agents at roughly $3,200 to $13,000 ([Azilen](https://www.azilen.com/blog/ai-agent-development-cost/)), while Groovy Web puts the running cost of a single-tool agent at $200 to $800 a month ([Groovy Web](https://www.groovyweb.co/blog/ai-agent-development-cost-guide-2026)). The gap is mostly people: who watches the agent, how often it is improved, and how much traffic it carries.

Three questions to ask any provider about running costs:

- **What is included, and what is billed per use?** Model tokens, phone minutes and messaging fees should be listed separately.
- **Who tunes the agent, and how often?** "Set and forget" usually means it gets worse over time.
- **What happens if you leave?** You should keep your data, prompts and configuration. With us you own the code and the setup.

## How to keep the cost down

- **Start with one job.** Pick the task with the clearest cost today, such as missed calls, slow lead replies or manual data entry. One agent that works is worth more than five in a pilot.
- **Buy packaged where you can.** Packaged agents have the scope, guardrails and tests already worked out. You pay for setup on your tools, not for design.
- **Let it draft before it acts.** Run the agent in shadow mode first: it proposes, a person approves. You learn where it is reliable before you trust it alone.
- **Clean your knowledge first.** Outdated prices and conflicting policies cost more to work around than to fix.
- **Get an outside view before a big build.** Our [AI Opportunity Audit](/services/ai-consulting/) costs {{audit}}, takes a week and is credited in full if you go ahead with a build within 60 days.

## Is an AI agent worth the cost?

Work it out with your own numbers. Take the job the agent will do and ask three things: how many times a month it happens, what one missed or slow case costs you, and how much staff time it takes. If the agent saves more than its monthly fee in recovered work or time, and the setup pays back within a few months, it is worth doing.

The opposite also happens. Gartner predicts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027 because of rising costs, unclear business value or weak risk controls ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). The way to stay out of that group is the same advice as above: one scoped job, a number to measure it against, and guardrails from day one.
