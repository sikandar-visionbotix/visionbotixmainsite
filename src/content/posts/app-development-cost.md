## What drives the cost of an app?

Our [project cost estimator](/estimate/) prices an app the same way we scope one: it starts from the "from" price for the app type and adds each driver you pick. These are the drivers, from the ones that move the price most:

1. **Platforms.** Each extra surface (a web app, a mobile app, a desktop app) adds a large share of the build, because each needs its own screens, testing and release process. A mobile app for only iOS or only Android costs a little less than both, because our mobile price already covers both from one codebase.
2. **Features.** User accounts and roles, payments or subscriptions, an admin dashboard, real-time chat or live updates, file uploads, reports, several languages, offline mode and moving data from an old system each add a fixed amount. Offline mode costs the most of these, followed by real-time features, an admin dashboard and data migration.
3. **Integrations.** Every system the app reads from or writes to (a CRM, an ERP, a payment provider, a booking engine) needs access rules, error handling and tests. The estimator prices them in steps: none, one or two, three to five, or six and more.
4. **AI features.** Search or chat over your own content is the smaller step. An AI agent that takes actions inside the app (updates records, books, sends) costs more, because it needs guardrails, approvals and evaluation.
5. **Design.** If we design it, design is a share of the build, never less than one [design sprint](/services/ui-ux-design/) ({{project:designSprint}}). If you already have tested designs, that line drops out.
6. **Sensitive data.** Health, finance or children's data adds security and compliance work: encryption, audit logs, data residency and access reviews.
7. **Stage and speed.** A prototype costs less than a first launch, and a product built for high traffic or audits from day one costs more. An "as soon as possible" date adds cost, because a larger team works in parallel.

The estimator shows a range, not a single number, because integrations and edge cases only become clear in scoping. The fixed quote after a call is the binding price.

Location is the biggest single lever on the price of the same app. TechnBrains, citing Accelerance's 2026 rate data, gives senior agency rates of $31 to $41 an hour in South and Southeast Asia, $60 to $75 in Latin America and $64 to $76 in Europe, and, separately, puts blended senior rates in the US, UK and Australia at $120 to $250 an hour ([TechnBrains](https://www.technbrains.com/blog/app-development-cost/)).

## What does an app cost after launch?

The build is not the last bill. Plan for four running costs:

- **Maintenance.** Operating system updates, library upgrades, security fixes and small improvements. Chop Dawg budgets 15–20% of the original build cost a year ([Chop Dawg](https://www.chopdawg.com/app-development-cost-2026/)), and Creole Studios puts it at 15–25% a year ([Creole Studios](https://www.creolestudios.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-make-an-app/)).
- **App store accounts.** The Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD per membership year, with fee waivers for some nonprofits, educational institutions and government bodies ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)). A Google Play developer account has a US$25 one-time registration fee ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en)).
- **Store commission.** If you sell inside the app, the stores take a share. Apple's Small Business Program charges a reduced 15% commission on paid apps and in-app purchases for developers with up to 1 million USD in proceeds ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). Google Play charges 15% on the first $1M of a developer's yearly earnings in many markets, while a newer fee structure, set by whether an install is new or existing, applies to users in Australia, the EEA, Japan, the UK and the US ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)).
- **Hosting and services.** Servers, databases, email and text messages, maps, analytics and any AI model usage. These scale with your users. We set up hosting in your own cloud account, so the provider bills you directly and we add nothing on top.

After the 30 days of included support, you can keep the app with us on a monthly plan or a dedicated team, or hand it to your own developers with the code and documentation.

## How can I cut the cost of building an app?

- **Build an MVP first.** Pick the one journey that proves the idea, ship it, and let real users tell you what to build next. The rest goes on a "later" list.
- **Use one codebase for iOS and Android.** React Native or Flutter covers both platforms with one team, so every feature is built once. Go native only when the app leans hard on device features or heavy graphics.
- **Start on the web.** If users mainly fill in forms, browse and get notifications, a progressive web app can come first, with mobile apps added later on the same back end.
- **Use a design system.** A shared set of components and styles makes every new screen faster to design and build, and keeps the app consistent as it grows.
- **Buy, don't build, the commodity parts.** Sign-in, payments, email and analytics come from established services. Spend the budget on what makes your product different.
- **Test with a prototype.** A clickable prototype in front of five or ten target users costs a fraction of rebuilding a feature nobody uses.
