An AI SDR beats a human SDR on cost, speed and consistency for the preparation work: researching accounts, building lists, writing first drafts and following up on time. A human SDR beats an AI SDR on relationships, complex discovery and judgment. For most B2B teams the right answer is a hybrid: the AI researches and drafts, a person approves the outreach and runs the conversations. This guide compares the real costs, what each does well and badly, and the email and LinkedIn rules that apply to both.

**An illustrative calculation, not a survey figure:** take the $80K median on-target earnings ([The Bridge Group](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)) and add benefits in the same ratio as the BLS private-industry average, where employers paid $14.07 in benefits for every $32.82 in wages, or about 43% on top of wages ([US BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm)). Ramp and turnover add a cost that does not show on a payslip. ## What does an AI SDR do well?

- **Research at scale.** Reading a company's website, news, job posts and your CRM history is slow work for a person, so it gets skipped when the list is long. An agent does it for every account on the list and writes the notes into your CRM.
- **List building and contact discovery.** It finds likely buyers and champions by role and verifies work emails through your data provider, so reps start from a clean list.
- **First drafts.** A specific first email tied to a real signal (a new location, an open role, a recent announcement) is the hardest part of outbound to do well at volume. AI writes the draft; your rep edits the parts that need a human voice.
- **Inbound lead research.** New sign-ups and demo requests are enriched with research, so your rep knows who they are talking to before the first call. For inbound leads that need an answer within 60 seconds by text, WhatsApp or call, the [Booking Agent](/ai-agents/ai-booking-agent/) is built for exactly that.
- **Follow-up consistency.** Follow-ups go out on schedule, "not now, try us in spring" becomes a dated task, and nothing depends on someone remembering.
- **Clean records.** Every note, email and reply is logged against the right account, a part of the job that is often left incomplete when reps are busy.

## What does an AI SDR do badly?

- **Relationships.** Buyers take meetings with people they trust. An AI can open the door; it cannot build the relationship that gets a champion to push your deal internally.
- **Complex discovery.** A good discovery call follows the buyer's answers, notices hesitation and asks the uncomfortable question. That is a person's job.
- **Judgment.** Knowing that an account is in the middle of layoffs, that a contact is a friend of your CEO, or that a message would land badly this week needs context an AI usually does not have.
- **Messaging that has not been tested.** An AI SDR repeats and scales what you give it. If your positioning is wrong, it sends the wrong message faster and to more people.
- **Volume as a goal.** Tools that make sending cheap encourage sending too much, which hurts your domain and your brand. The fix is a person deciding who should get a message at all.

## When is a human SDR the better choice?

Hire a person, or keep the one you have, when:

- **deals are large and few.** If you sell to a short list of enterprise accounts, each message deserves a person's full attention, and research tools support the rep rather than replace them;
- **the job is mostly conversations.** Cold calling, event networking and multi-threading a buying committee are human work;
- **you are building a sales team.** The SDR role is also how many companies train future account executives, and an AI does not grow into one;
- **your market is small or relationship-driven.** In a niche where everyone knows everyone, one bad automated email costs more than a month of an AI's output saves.

Choose an AI SDR first when your bottleneck is preparation (research, lists, drafts and follow-up), your ideal customer is clear, and the people who should be talking to buyers are spending their day on admin instead.

## What are the deliverability and compliance risks?

Using AI does not change the rules: the same email and data protection laws apply whether a person or a model wrote the message. What follows is general information, not legal advice; confirm your approach with a legal advisor for each market you sell into.

**Deliverability.** Mailbox providers judge your sending, not your tool. Google requires all senders to set up SPF or DKIM, requires bulk senders (more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts) to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC and to support one-click unsubscribe on marketing messages, and expects spam rates reported in Postmaster Tools to stay below 0.3%, with a recommended target below 0.1% ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Steady daily limits, warmed-up inboxes, suppression lists and emails relevant to the reader protect you more than any writing trick.

**United States (CAN-SPAM).** The FTC's guide says the law "makes no exception for business-to-business email". Commercial emails need accurate sender details, honest subject lines, a valid physical postal address and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, and each violating email can cost up to $53,088 in penalties ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). You stay responsible even when a tool or agency sends for you.

**United Kingdom (PECR and UK GDPR).** The consent rule for marketing email in regulation 22 of PECR applies to individual subscribers, so emails to corporate subscribers, such as a limited company's staff, are not covered by that consent rule ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22)). Regulation 2 defines an individual as a living individual, including an unincorporated body of individuals, so sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2)). Data protection law still applies to a named person's work email, so you need a lawful basis, a clear sender and an easy opt-out.

**European Union (GDPR and national ePrivacy laws).** GDPR accepts that direct marketing "may be regarded as carried out for a legitimate interest" ([GDPR Recital 47](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-47/)), but the recipient has the right to object at any time, and once they do, their data can no longer be used for marketing ([GDPR Article 21](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-21-gdpr/)). The ePrivacy Directive's consent rule for marketing email applies to subscribers who are natural persons and leaves each member state to protect businesses as recipients ([ePrivacy Directive, Article 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02002L0058-20091219)), so national rules for business addresses differ. Check each country you target.

**LinkedIn.** LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids using "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). Some outreach tools still offer LinkedIn automation; the risk falls on the rep whose profile gets restricted. Our SDR Agent **only drafts** LinkedIn messages and connection notes for your reps to send themselves. It never automates LinkedIn accounts.

## What does a hybrid AI and human SDR model look like?

The hybrid model gives each side the work it does best:

1. **AI researches.** The agent reads each target account and writes research notes and a suggested angle into your CRM.
2. **AI drafts.** It writes a short first email and follow-ups for each contact, using only the claims and proof points you have approved.
3. **A person approves.** Your rep reviews, edits and approves each sequence. Nothing is sent until they do, and you can require approval for every individual email.
4. **AI sends and sorts.** Approved emails go out in small batches within your daily limits. Replies are sorted (interested, not now, wrong person, unsubscribe), simple questions are answered and meetings are booked into the right calendar.
5. **A person runs the conversation.** Your rep or account executive takes the meeting, handles the discovery and builds the relationship.

## How do you compare results fairly?

We know of no reliable independent benchmark for AI SDR results, and vendor figures come from their own customers and are not independently verified. Compare on your own numbers instead:

- **Run a pilot.** Give the AI one segment of accounts and your SDR (or founder) a similar segment, for the same weeks.
- **Measure meetings held, not emails sent.** Sends and opens are easy to inflate; meetings held and qualified pipeline are not.
- **Count cost per meeting held.** Divide each option's full monthly cost, including the people reviewing drafts, by meetings held.
- **Watch the health metrics.** Bounce rate, spam complaints and unsubscribes tell you whether the volume is costing you your domain.
- **Read the replies.** Negative replies about tone or relevance are an early sign the messaging needs work, whoever wrote it.
