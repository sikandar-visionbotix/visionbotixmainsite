Pakistan usually has the lowest published hourly rates of the three, India has by far the largest market and the biggest vendors, and Eastern Europe costs more but works in European hours and, inside the EU, under European data law. A disclosure first: Visionbotix is a software studio in Rawalpindi, Pakistan, so we have a stake in this answer. We have tried to be fair, and the sections below say plainly where India or Eastern Europe is the better pick.

## How big is each talent market, and how good is the English?

**India is in a different league on scale.** Nasscom estimates India's tech industry revenue (IT services, BPM, engineering R&D, software products and hardware) at $315 billion in FY26, with export revenue of $246 billion ([Nasscom](https://d3r3sr3o54kk15.cloudfront.net/s3fs-public/media_pdf/India%E2%80%99s%20tech%20industry%20continues%20to%20redefine%20AI-led%20transformation%2C%20efficiency%20and%20skilling--%20Nasscom%20Annual%20Strategic%20Review%202026.pdf)). If you need a hundred engineers, a niche enterprise platform or a vendor with offices on three continents, India has more options than anywhere else.

**Pakistan is smaller but growing fast.** IT exports reached a record $4.6 billion in FY2025-26, up 21% from $3.814 billion the year before, according to State Bank of Pakistan balance-of-payments data ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2618902/it-exports-hit-record-46b-1)). The market is made of many small and mid-sized studios rather than a few giants, which suits product teams that want senior people close to the work.

**Eastern Europe is a set of separate markets.** Ukraine's computer services exports grew 3.3% to $6.66 billion in 2025, according to National Bank of Ukraine data, despite the war ([Lviv IT Cluster](https://itcluster.lviv.ua/en/gradual-market-stabilization-what-tech-services-exports-looked-like-in-2025/)). Poland and Romania add large engineering communities inside the EU.

On English, the EF English Proficiency Index 2026 is based on test results from 2.3 million adults in 117 countries and regions, and ranks Romania 12th (593) and Poland 15th (587) ([EF](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/)), Ukraine 41st (524) ([EF](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/regions/europe/ukraine/)), Pakistan 58th (496) ([EF](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/regions/asia/pakistan/)) and India 69th (479) ([EF](https://www.ef.com/wwen/epi/regions/asia/india/)). It measures adults who took EF's test, not the engineers you will hire. In South Asia, English is the working language of universities and the software industry, so the people on your calls usually speak it daily. Still, judge the actual team on a call, not the country average.

## How many hours overlap with the US, UK and EU?

Overlap decides how fast questions get answered, so it matters more than most buyers expect. Pakistan is UTC+5 and India UTC+5:30, and neither changes the clocks. Poland is UTC+1, and Romania and Ukraine are UTC+2, and they move their clocks in summer as the UK and most of Europe do. With both sides on a normal 09:00 to 17:00 day, in winter:

| Team in | London (GMT) | Berlin (CET) | New York (EST) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pakistan (UTC+5) | 3 hours (09:00–12:00 London) | 4 hours | None |
| India (UTC+5:30) | 2.5 hours | 3.5 hours | None |
| Poland (UTC+1) | 7 hours | The whole day | 2 hours |
| Romania, Ukraine (UTC+2) | 6 hours | 7 hours | 1 hour |

In summer, Pakistan and India gain one hour with the UK and Europe because only the European clocks move.

For European buyers, that is the strongest argument for Eastern Europe: the whole team is online when you are, with no special arrangement. For US buyers, no region overlaps much on a normal day, so everything depends on whether the vendor works a shifted day.

Projects for UK clients run from 12:00 to 21:00 Pakistan time, which is 07:00 to 16:00 in London in winter (seven hours of overlap) and the whole working day during British Summer Time; see [our UK page](/locations/uk/), which computes the overlap live. ## What about contracts, IP and data transfers?

**IP and code ownership.** In every country, the contract should assign all code, designs, prompts and data to you, and the vendor's own employment contracts must assign their staff's work to the vendor first, or the chain breaks. Keep the code in a repository you own from day one, so ownership is a fact and not only a clause.

**EU personal data.** Poland and Romania are EU members, so a team there works under the GDPR with no international transfer. Pakistan, India and Ukraine are outside the EU, so if the team can access personal data of people in the EU, you need a transfer tool. The usual one is the standard contractual clauses, model terms pre-approved by the European Commission and modernized in June 2021 ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/standard-contractual-clauses-scc_en)), plus a transfer impact assessment.

**UK personal data.** The UK treats EU countries as adequate, so Polish and Romanian teams are straightforward for UK buyers. For Pakistan and India, UK exporters use the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or the UK Addendum to the EU clauses, with a transfer risk assessment. Our [UK page](/locations/uk/) describes what we sign.

**Local law in the vendor's country.** India passed its Digital Personal Data Protection Act in 2023. Pakistan's data protection bill is still a draft, so Pakistani teams work under PECA and sector rules, plus whatever your contract requires; [our Pakistan page](/locations/pakistan/) explains how we fill that gap.

The simplest risk control works in every country: build with test data and give engineers access to live records only when needed, with logged access. Then most of the transfer question disappears.

## What are the infrastructure and stability risks?

Every region has one, and a good vendor will name it before you ask.

**Pakistan: power and internet.** Some areas still have scheduled power cuts, and the internet has been disrupted at national level. Top10VPN estimated Pakistan's 2024 losses from internet disruptions at $1.62 billion, with 9,735 hours of disruptions affecting 82.9 million users ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/01/06/pakistan-faces-1-62bn-losses-from-internet-disruptions-it-sector-calls-for-urgent-5g-rollout)). Ask any Pakistani vendor how they handle this, and expect a specific answer: backup power for the office and staff at home, two internet providers on different networks, a mobile data fallback, code and environments in the cloud rather than on a laptop, and written handover notes so a cut delays a day's work, not a release.

**India: who you actually get.** India has the most large vendors, and the risk with any large vendor, anywhere, is staffing: senior people can win the deal while the account is staffed with juniors, or engineers rotate between accounts. Name the people in the contract and agree how replacements are approved.

**Eastern Europe: the war.** Ukrainian teams have kept delivering, as the export figures above show, but plan for air-raid alerts, power cuts after strikes on the grid and staff who may relocate or be called up. Polish and Romanian teams carry less of this risk at a higher rate.

## When is India or Eastern Europe the better choice?

Choose **Eastern Europe** when:

- you are an EU company and want personal data to stay inside the EU without transfer paperwork;
- your team needs the whole working day together, every day, with no shifted hours;
- you sell into regulated EU sectors where buyers prefer EU-based suppliers;
- budget matters less than the shortest feedback loop.

Choose **India** when:

- you need a large team quickly, or many teams across several programs;
- your stack is a niche enterprise platform with a small talent pool elsewhere;
- you want a large vendor with formal delivery processes and a global footprint.

Choose **Pakistan** when:

- you want senior engineers for a product team at a lower rate and can work with a shifted day;
- you want a smaller studio where the founder and lead stay close to the work;
- your work is AI agents, web and mobile apps or SaaS, where small senior teams do well.

If you are unsure, test before you commit: one paid, fixed-price milestone with two vendors tells you more than any country comparison.

## How do you choose a vendor in any country?

Use this checklist whichever country you pick:

1. **Paid discovery first.** A short, paid scoping phase with a written output (scope, architecture, risks, fixed quote) shows how a vendor thinks before you commit to a build.
2. **IP clauses.** All code, designs, prompts and data assigned to you, and the vendor confirms its staff contracts assign their work to the vendor.
3. **Code ownership in practice.** Your GitHub or GitLab organization, your cloud accounts, your domain, from day one. Access removed the day someone leaves.
4. **Overlap hours in writing.** The exact hours, in your time zone, for standups and urgent fixes, and who answers outside them.
5. **Named people.** You interview the engineers and approve any replacement.
6. **References you can call.** Ask for two past clients you can speak to, not only logos or written quotes.
7. **Data handling.** Test data by default, the transfer tool your law requires, and where the data is hosted.
8. **Exit terms.** Notice period, handover of documentation and a recorded walkthrough when the work ends.
