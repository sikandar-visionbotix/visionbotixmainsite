faq:
  - q: What is the difference between UI and UX design?
    a: UX design is how the product works, such as the steps a user takes, what they see first and how they recover from mistakes. UI design is how it looks, such as layout, type, color and components. You need both, and we do both in one team.
  - q: What is a design sprint for one product flow?
    a: It is a short, fixed-scope engagement that takes one important journey, such as onboarding, checkout or an AI approval flow, from research to a tested, clickable prototype. It is the fastest way to fix a weak flow or to see how we work before a larger project.
  - q: Do you only design, or can you build it too?
    a: Both. You can take the Figma files and the component library to your own developers, or we can build the product with our web and mobile teams, so nothing is lost between design and code.
  - q: Can you redesign our product without confusing existing users?
    a: Yes. We find the screens that cause the most friction, redesign those first and roll changes out in stages, often behind a toggle for early users. Familiar patterns stay where they work.
  - q: How do you design for AI features and agents?
    a: We make the AI's work visible. Users see what the agent is doing, which sources it used and what it plans to change, and anything risky waits for their approval. We also design how users correct the AI, because trust comes from being able to undo.
  - q: Will the design be accessible?
    a: Yes. We design to WCAG AA by default, covering contrast, focus states, keyboard paths, touch target sizes and screen reader labels, and we note these in the handoff so they survive the build.
  - q: Do we own the design files?
    a: Yes. The Figma files, components, icons made for you and all design IP are yours on payment, in your own Figma workspace if you prefer.

## Template vs freelance designer vs product design team

| Compared on | UI template or kit | Freelance designer | Product design team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | A quick internal tool or a first demo | A defined set of screens | A product you want users to adopt and keep |
| Research and usability testing | None | Sometimes | Built into the process |
| Fits your users and workflow | Generic | Yes, for the screens in scope | Yes, across the whole product |
| Design system and handoff | Whatever the kit includes | Varies | Components matched to code |
| Design for AI features | Rarely | Depends on experience | Approvals, sources and undo designed in |
| Continuity as the product grows | You adapt it yourself | Depends on availability | A named lead and a team |
